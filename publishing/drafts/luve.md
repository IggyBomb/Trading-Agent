The shares closed at EUR 62.50 today, up 8.3% on the session, as the board
approved the H1 2026 financial report and management held its scheduled
analyst presentation. That move sits inside a wider round trip: the stock
is still roughly 16% below where it traded immediately after the Q2 print,
and about 13% below its 52-week high of EUR 71.60.

The case for the rating. Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.4% year-on-year with
adjusted EBITDA up 16.9% and net income up 45%; Q2 revenue reached a
quarterly record, up 8.2%. The order backlog stood at EUR 333.3 million at
30 June 2026, up 48.1% year-on-year and a record for the group. Management
raised full-year revenue guidance to EUR 4.00-4.15 billion at the Q2 print.
None of that is priced as a surprise today — Intermonte's EUR 71.40 target,
set before the H1 print, already assumed continued backlog conversion.

What the discount to that target is pricing. The stock trades at a forward
P/E of 21.9x on consensus forward EPS of EUR 2.85, against a trailing
33.2x — a multiple that already embeds a large step-up in earnings. At the
Q2 report, shares fell as much as 13.4% in after-hours despite the top and
bottom-line beat, on margin-mix concerns within the E-Infrastructure
segment and second-half revenue guidance that management itself described
as conservative on project timing and weather assumptions. That reaction is
the market's own statement that growth alone does not clear the bar at
this valuation — mix and guidance quality matter as much as the growth
rate.

Thesis invalidation. The case rests on backlog-to-revenue conversion
holding at the H1 pace without further margin compression. A second-half
print showing that conversion slowing, or margins compressing beyond what
was already flagged at the Q2 call, is the point at which the growth
narrative would need restating, not defending.
