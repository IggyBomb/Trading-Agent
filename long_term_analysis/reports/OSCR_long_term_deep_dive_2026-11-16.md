## 1. Business Overview

**Segments.** Oscar has a single reporting segment: health insurance plans sold on the ACA individual marketplaces, including ICHRA/CHOICE employer-funded individual plans. It also has a small +Oscar technology platform and broker/enrollment services. Revenue is mostly premium income net of risk adjustment, plus investment income.

**Market.**
- US only, across about 20 states on the federal and state marketplaces.
- Customers are low-to-middle-income individuals (100–400% FPL), sold mainly through brokers and direct enrollment.
- Competitors: Centene (Ambetter, the largest ACA player), Molina, UnitedHealthcare, Elevance, Cigna, BCBS plans, and Aetna (exited the ACA for 2026).
- Oscar is among the largest by membership: 2.69M effectuated members.

**TAM.** The ACA marketplace had about 24M enrollees at its 2025 peak, likely lower in 2026 after the eAPTCs expired. Management's Investor Day estimate is an addressable individual market of about 16M people by 2029 (secondary source, not verified in the deck). Penetration is 2.69M of ~20–24M today, roughly **11–13%** share.

## 2. Routing

**A-HC (Track A, Healthcare Services / managed care).** The company is a risk-bearing insurer, so value comes from underwriting margin on premiums plus investment income on the float. Generic EV-based multiples are distorted by the $8.6B of regulated cash and investments, so the valuation uses equity value, P/E and P/B.

## 3. Lynch category

**Turnaround with Cyclical traits.**
- Net margin: −14.7% (2022), −4.6% (2023), +0.3% (2024), −3.8% (2025), +4.9% (TTM). 2026 is the first real profit year.
- ACA pricing runs in cycles: losses in 2022–23 and 2025 as morbidity and subsidies shifted.
- PEG is not meaningful. Use Through-Cycle and Years of Consensus instead.

## 4. Earnings quality (Penman)

- **Accrual ratio (TTM)** = (NI $855M − OCF $4,183M) / average assets $8.17B = **−41%**. Cash runs far ahead of earnings. For an insurer this is mostly timing:
  - premiums are received in advance;
  - claims are payable later;
  - the risk-adjustment payable is due to CMS the following summer.

  So OCF is not "free" cash: Q3-26 OCF was −$1.2B as those timing items reversed.
- **ROE** TTM ≈ 53% on average equity of about $1.6B. That is high because equity was small after years of losses, not because the business is structurally high-return. ROE in 2025 was −45%.
- **Margin trend (TTM figures):** EBIT margin 5.3% vs −3.6% in FY25. The MLR improvement is real (pricing), but $250M of RA true-up and $42M of prior-period development flatter 2026.
- **Dilution:** basic shares 250.5M (Dec-24) → 297.7M (Dec-25) → 310.6M (Sep-26), about +11% a year. Q3 diluted average is 346.5M including the 2030 converts and awards. SBC is about $75–90M a year.
- **Non-GAAP:** adjusted EBITDA ($1,349M for 9M) is close to GAAP earnings from operations ($1,271M), so there's no large add-back gap.

### Method

- Equity FCFE = net income − 7% capital retained per $ of revenue growth. This is about the statutory capital level ($2.3B on ~$19B) plus a buffer.
- Explicit period 2027–2033 (narrow-moat CAP), terminal growth 3%.
- Tax: NOLs shelter 2027–28, then 24%.
- Add year-end 2026 excess capital of about $0.96B ($2.76/sh): statutory $2.3B − 7% × $19.06B − the Q4 loss + parent cash.
